## What happened

Job `xltwdet-v1` (Dell, 2026-11-07 12:03-12:06 UTC) stopped in its own unit tests, before any of the 15 study runs
in [expectations.md](expectations.md). The package's 21 tests passed; in `test_det.py` 15 of 16 passed and the
end-to-end test failed: three runs of `detsolver.py` (0.1.0-det1) on one synthetic 80-customer instance with seed 3
and a 10 s limit did not end at the same solution (the first two, both without the CPU hog, printed final costs
2,143 and 2,660). No study run took place, so D0-D6 are neither scored nor changed.

## Why, as far as the evidence goes

PyVRP 0.14.1 never reads the clock: `solve()` seeds one generator, and the iterated local search calls its stopping
criterion once before each iteration (checked in PyVRP's source, `pyvrp/solve.py` and
`pyvrp/IteratedLocalSearch.py` at tag v0.14.1). So the same seed and the same number of iterations should give the
same solution, and the difference must come from where the runs stopped. The likely cause is our budget, not PyVRP:
the rate model 300 x (n / 1000) ** -0.5 it/s was fitted at n = 1,047-8,574, and at n = 80 it extrapolates to about
1,060 it/s. With safety 0.6 that is a budget of about 5,400 iterations in 8.5 s, and the three runs and the rest of
the test took 26 s together, about the 3 x 8.5 s that three clock stops would take. So the runs most likely
reached the wall-clock safety net (`clock_stop`) at different points. The test compared solutions before it checked
`clock_stop`, so its log cannot confirm this. That ordering was the test's fault.

## What changes (and what does not)

- `detsolver.py` becomes **0.1.0-det2**: the only difference is that the environment variable `XLTW_DET_SAFETY`,
  if set, replaces the safety factor 0.6. It is for the unit test. The study's runs do not set it (a new test checks
  that the job's environment lacks it), and every stats line records the safety used. The budget formula, its
  constants, the method and the harness are unchanged.
- `test_det.py`: the end-to-end test now runs with `XLTW_DET_SAFETY=0.05` and a 20 s limit (about 980 iterations),
  checks every run's stats first (no clock stop, no SIGTERM, bound not cut short) and prints them, then compares the
  solutions. It is preceded by a control that runs PyVRP alone twice (same data, seed 3, exactly 400 iterations)
  and requires identical results.
- `detrun.py` is used exactly as published at commit b7b7755. `expectations.md` (D0-D6) is unchanged, and the job
  checks that it is byte-identical on `main` to its first published version. If any run's stats show a safety other
  than 0.6, that run is invalid.
- If the new end-to-end test fails with no clock stop and no bound cut-off, the cause is not the budget, and the
  study does not run until that is understood.

## A finding in its own right (descriptive)

A work budget set from n and the limit alone can fall back to the wall clock when the rate model is extrapolated
outside the sizes it was fitted on. D3 (budget runs ending at 30-85% of the limit) tests the model where the
challenge uses it (n >= 1,047). For tiny instances the solver should cap the rate estimate. That is left for a later
version, because XLTW-Test has no instance below 1,047 customers.
